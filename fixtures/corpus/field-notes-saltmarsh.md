# Day 1 — Arrival at the estuary {#day-1 .fieldday}

We reached the station a little after nine. The tide was already on the
turn, so the first transect (T1) had to wait until the afternoon. Mateus
spent the morning recalibrating the *refractometer*, which had drifted by
almost two units over the winter.

Weather on arrival:\
overcast, wind from the northwest,\
air temperature 11 °C.

The boardwalk near the old sluice gate has partly collapsed. We will need
to route around it, which adds roughly forty metres to each walk out to
plots P7–P9.

## Transect T1 {#t1}

- Start point marked with the orange stake, GPS fix taken twice
- Quadrats every 5 m, 1 m² each
  - Percent cover of *Spartina* estimated by eye
  - *Salicornia* counted individually where sparse
- Sediment cores at 0 m, 25 m and 50 m

Notes on the cores: the 25 m core was noticeably darker and smelled of
sulphide, which fits with the standing water we saw there in February.
The 50 m core was sandier than expected.

## Transect T2 {#t2 .partial}

We only got halfway along T2 before the light went. The quadrats we did
complete are listed below; the rest will be done tomorrow morning.

* Q1–Q4: dense *Spartina*, little bare mud
* Q5: a shallow pan, mostly bare, with a thin algal film
* Q6: abrupt change to *Atriplex* and *Elytrigia* on a slight rise

Day 2 — Tidal creek survey
--------------------------

Early start. Low water was at 06:42, which gave us a good two hours in
the creek before it became unsafe. Joana led this part; she has walked
this creek every spring since 2019.

> The creek edge has moved again. Last year the slumped block was on the
> outside of the bend; now it is right in the channel.
>
> — Joana, standing at the second bend

We photographed the slump from both banks and took a fix on each end.
The block is about 3 m long. Whether it stays put depends on the next
spring tides.

![Slumped bank block at the second bend](img/creek-slump.jpg)

### Water samples

Salinity readings, taken at the surface with the recalibrated meter:

1. Creek mouth: 31 ‰
2. First bend: 28 ‰
3. Second bend: 24 ‰
4. Upper creek, below the culvert: 17 ‰

The gradient is steeper than last year. One possible reason is the
heavier rainfall in March; another is that the culvert was cleared in
the winter and now passes more freshwater. We should ask the council
whether that work happened.

### Birds

Not part of the protocol, but worth noting: a pair of avocets on the
pan near Q5, and a large flock of dunlin (est. 200+) on the mudflat at
low water. Margarida thinks the count is low for April.

Day 3 — Remaining quadrats and pack-up
======================================

Finished T2 by ten. The pan at Q5 had more water in it than on Tuesday,
so the cover estimate there should be treated with some caution.

Things to bring next time:

- Spare batteries for the GPS (the second unit died on day 2)
- A longer core tube for the sandy plots
- Waders in the larger size — the borrowed pair leaked

Things to follow up:

- Ask Dr. Müller about the sulphide smell at 25 m on T1
- Send the photo set to the reserve warden
- Check whether the culvert was cleared, and when

Back at the university by six. Samples are in the cold room, shelf C,
labelled with the date and transect code.
