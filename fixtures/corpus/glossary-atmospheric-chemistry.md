# Glossary of Terms {#glossary}

This glossary accompanies the second-year module *Introduction to
Atmospheric Chemistry*. Definitions are deliberately short; follow the
links for fuller treatment. Units follow SI conventions, with
concentrations in mol m^−3^ unless stated otherwise.

## Gases and species

Aerosol
:   A suspension of fine solid or liquid particles in a gas. In
    atmospheric science the term usually refers to the particles
    themselves.

Hydroxyl radical
:   OH, the main daytime oxidant in the troposphere. Often called the
    "detergent of the atmosphere" because it initiates the removal of
    many trace gases, including CH~4~ and CO.

Ozone
:   O~3~. Beneficial in the stratosphere, where it absorbs UV-B; a
    pollutant at ground level, where it harms lungs and crops.

Nitrogen oxides
:   NO and NO~2~, together written NO~x~. Produced mainly by
    combustion. Central to ground-level ozone formation.

Volatile organic compounds
:   Organic species with a high vapour pressure at room temperature,
    abbreviated VOCs. Sources include vegetation (isoprene, terpenes)
    and solvents.

## Quantities and units

Mixing ratio

:   The amount of a species per amount of air, usually expressed as
    parts per million (ppm) or parts per billion (ppb). Unlike
    concentration, a mixing ratio does not change when air is
    compressed or expands.

    For CO~2~, the global mean mixing ratio passed 420 ppm in 2023.

Number density

:   Molecules per unit volume, typically in molecules cm^−3^. At sea
    level, air has a number density of about 2.5 × 10^19^ molecules
    cm^−3^.

Lifetime

:   The average time a molecule remains in the atmosphere before being
    removed. For a species removed by a first-order process with rate
    constant *k*, the lifetime is 1/*k*.

    Lifetimes range from seconds (OH) to centuries (some
    perfluorocarbons).

Dobson unit
:   A unit of total column ozone. One DU corresponds to a layer of
    pure ozone 10 µm thick at standard temperature and pressure. See
    the [NASA explainer][dobson] for a picture.

## Processes

Photolysis
:   Breaking of a chemical bond by absorption of a photon. Ozone
    photolysis, O~3~ + hν → O~2~ + O(^1^D), is the main source of OH.

Dry deposition
:   Removal of gases and particles at the surface without
    precipitation.

Wet deposition
:   Removal by rain, snow or fog. The dominant sink for soluble species
    such as HNO~3~ and SO~4~^2−^.

Ozone depletion
:   Chemical destruction of stratospheric ozone, principally by
    chlorine and bromine released from CFCs and halons. Regulated under
    the [Montreal Protocol][montreal].

## Further reading

The standard textbook is [Seinfeld and Pandis][sp], which covers
everything here in much more depth. The [IUPAC Gold Book] is the
authority on terminology. For a gentler introduction, see Jacob's
*Introduction to Atmospheric Chemistry*, which is [freely available
online][jacob].
