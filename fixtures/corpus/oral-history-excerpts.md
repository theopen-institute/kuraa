# About these excerpts

The passages below are taken from interviews recorded between 2019 and
2022 for the community archive. Interviewees chose whether to be named;
where they preferred not to be, initials are used. Line breaks in the
song and poem extracts follow the speaker's own phrasing, not any
published version.

# Interview 7: R. Takahashi

Recorded at the community centre, October 2021. R.T. came to the city
in 1968 to work in a textile mill and later ran a small grocery. She
switched between English and Japanese throughout; the Japanese passages
are given with a translation.

> My mother wrote to me every month. The first letter, I still have it.
> She wrote at the end:
>
> | 元気でいてね。
> | 寒くなったら、ちゃんと着込むこと。
>
> "Stay well. When it gets cold, dress properly." That's all. Every
> letter ended like that.

She described the mill as loud and hot in summer, and remembered that
the foreman, a man called Mr. Ó Briain, learned a few words of Japanese
so he could greet the new workers.

# Interview 12: José Álvarez Núñez

Recorded at his home, March 2020. José arrived from Galicia in 1962
and worked on building sites, then as a lorry driver for twenty-three
years.

He sang part of a song his father used to sing while they were
waiting at the port:

| Adiós, ríos; adiós, fontes;
| adiós, regatos pequenos;
| adiós, vista dos meus ollos,
|     non sei cando nos veremos.

"It's Rosalía de Castro," he said. "Everybody knew it. Even people who
couldn't read knew it."

Asked about his first winter, he laughed:

> Cold! I didn't know cold like that. In Galicia it rains, yes, but
> this — the first morning there was ice on the inside of the window.
> I thought the glass was broken.

# Interview 15: A.K.

Recorded by telephone, January 2022. A.K. asked that her name and the
names of her employers be withheld. She worked as a nurse from 1971.

> We were twelve on the ward, and four of us from overseas. The sister
> in charge was very strict but she was fair. If you did the work, she
> didn't care where you came from. That wasn't true everywhere.

She recited a few lines she had written in a notebook during her first
year, and gave permission for them to be included:

| Night shift. The corridor hums.
| Somebody's radio, very low,
| and the tea trolley at four,
| and then the long walk home in the dark
|   past the bakery, already warm.

# A note on transcription

Pauses longer than about two seconds are marked with an ellipsis.
Words the transcriber could not make out are marked *[inaudible]*.
Non-English passages are transcribed in the original script and
followed by a translation agreed with the interviewee where possible.
